# C — Semantic Fit Thesis

Status: canonical thesis for Workstream C during architecture migration.

## Question C answers

For each opportunity from G, judge only:

> **Is the actual day-to-day content of this role genuinely the kind of work the user wants to do?**

C does **not** decide whether the role is currently actionable. Ignore geography, salary, language requirements, link health, work authorization, company rating, country weights and H attainability. Those belong downstream.

## Output scale

### Strong

Use **Strong only when the core day-to-day job is genuinely target work**, not because the description happens to mention a relevant keyword.

Typical Strong lanes:
- corporate treasury with real execution: liquidity/funding, FX, rates, commodities, hedging, derivatives, financing, capital markets, cash/liquidity management;
- practical market/liquidity/financial-risk roles closely linked to markets, hedging, derivatives, positions, scenarios or treasury decisions;
- M&A / corporate finance / deal finance with substantive transaction execution, valuation, modelling, structuring, due diligence or financing;
- corporate development where transactions are a meaningful core responsibility;
- investment roles involving actual underwriting, security/company analysis, investment decisions, portfolio construction or direct-investment work;
- restructuring / capital advisory with substantive modelling, liquidity, financing or transaction work;
- transaction-oriented valuation/modelling where the analysis directly supports deals or investment decisions.

### Moderate

Use **Moderate when the role is relevant and plausible, but the attractive work is secondary, diluted or adjacent rather than the core**.

Typical Moderate lanes:
- generic FP&A, strategic finance or finance business partnering without a strong treasury/deal/investment angle;
- portfolio monitoring, investment controls or investment-adjacent operations without real investment decision-making;
- credit-heavy roles unless markets/structured-finance/investment content is clearly central;
- pure quant/model-development roles where modelling itself is the profession rather than a tool for practical finance decisions;
- generic controlling, performance management or CFO support;
- trading roles that are product-specific or execution-only without a broader analytical/treasury/investment fit;
- finance strategy/advisory that is interesting but lacks direct execution or sufficiently substantive finance analysis.

### Weak

Use **Weak when the actual profession is materially outside the target**, even if the employer or title sounds finance-adjacent.

Typical Weak lanes:
- reporting, bookkeeping, statutory accounting, audit or assurance;
- compliance, regulatory control, non-financial risk or forensics;
- finance transformation where systems/process implementation is the core job;
- ERP/SAP implementation, IT, software, data engineering, data architecture or analytics engineering;
- sales, relationship management, client acquisition, investor relations or outreach-led roles;
- pure operations, project management or organisational-performance work;
- tax / transfer pricing;
- wealth-management/private-banking roles dominated by client coverage;
- non-finance roles.

## Decision rules that prevent false Strong ratings

1. **Judge responsibilities, not titles or keywords.** "Treasury", "M&A", "investment", "risk" or "markets" in a title does not by itself justify Strong.
2. **Core-duty test.** Ask what the person would spend most of the week doing. Strong requires target work to be central, not incidental.
3. **Execution matters.** Prefer roles where analysis leads to financing, hedging, transaction, investment or market decisions over roles that only report, monitor or coordinate.
4. **M&A mention is not enough.** A business-controller/strategic-finance role that occasionally supports M&A is normally Moderate, not Strong.
5. **Markets adjacency is not enough.** Credit risk, prudential liquidity, controls or product operations do not become Strong merely because they interact with Markets/Treasury.
6. **Investment adjacency is not enough.** Controls, guidelines, reporting and operations are not investment decision-making.
7. **Quant is a tool, not the desired profession.** Applied modelling in treasury/deals/investments can be Strong; deep model-development/code-heavy quant as the core role is normally Moderate and can be Weak when it is effectively a software/quant-dev job.
8. **Trading is not automatically Strong.** Product-specific trading can be Moderate if the content is mainly execution/market-making and does not match the broader target work. Treat genuine analytical portfolio/investment decision-making more positively.
9. **Seniority is mostly downstream.** Do not downgrade C merely because a description asks for 5–7 years. Only let seniority affect C when the actual content changes into leadership/origination/people-management rather than the target analytical/execution work.
10. **Company attractiveness never changes C.** A great employer cannot turn weak role content into Strong.

## Positive calibration precedents

Patterns repeatedly supported by user decisions:
- hands-on corporate treasury, especially FX/rates/commodities/hedging/liquidity/funding;
- treasury advisory when it remains finance/markets-led rather than ERP implementation;
- corporate development with genuine M&A/valuation/transaction work;
- capital advisory/restructuring with modelling, liquidity and financing;
- structured/acquisition finance with real transaction analysis;
- market/liquidity risk with derivatives, hedging, scenario/stress work and practical market exposure;
- direct investment / portfolio-management roles with real investment decisions.

## Negative calibration precedents

Patterns repeatedly rejected or questioned:
- "too quant" / too code-heavy;
- "too outreach" / communicator or sales-heavy rather than analyst work;
- generic business analyst / commercial business-partner roles rather than finance execution;
- credit-heavy roles when credit is the dominant profession;
- pure bond/product trading is not automatically attractive merely because it is markets-facing;
- investment roles lacking execution/decision-making;
- pure IT/data/analytics engineering;
- compliance, accounting, reporting, operations, tax and project-management-heavy work.

## Feedback learning

J feedback and actual I outcomes may provide evidence for changing this thesis, but **C Work must not silently rewrite the thesis while judging roles**.

A thesis change should be explicit and versioned when repeated feedback shows a durable preference pattern. Individual salary, language, location, link-health or seniority comments are not C-thesis evidence unless the comment clearly concerns the work itself.
